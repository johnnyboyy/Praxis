# Phase: writing-draft

Produce a first draft of a prose product — a story, an article, a piece of copy, a contract clause, a
doc page — for a stated genre and style. The **divergent** half of the writing process: it opens the
space and gets words down, before the convergent `writing-revision` pass narrows and tightens them.

This phase is deliberately **process, not craft**. It lays out *how a writing task moves* — pick the
genre and style, compose whatever writing judgment the project has earned for that genre, draft, then
hand the draft to revision. It does **not** carry rules about what makes good fiction or enforceable
legal prose: that judgment is not pre-authored here, because a baked-in "good writing" rule is just
the baseline craft an agent already applies, and corpora only earns its keep with judgment that beats
baseline. Such judgment accretes at the ratify gate as real writing surfaces it (see "Surfaced").

**Entry condition:** a task whose deliverable is a prose product, tagged with a **genre** and a
**style**. Genre is one of the recognized writing units-of-work — `write-fiction`, `write-nonfiction`,
`write-copy`, `write-legal`, `write-documentation` (the set is extensible; a genre the work needs but
this list lacks is itself a finding). Style is the cross-cutting register the piece must hold (voice,
formality, person, tense) — named in the brief, not chosen here.

**Stance:** divergent — a first draft explores; it is not the place to converge on the final wording.

**Invocations:** the judgment engine composed for the piece's genre unit-of-work (`write-<genre>`),
carrying whatever genre and style domains the project has accrued. Early on that composition is thin
or empty — the phase still runs; the draft is produced on the agent's own competence, and what the
gate later keeps is only the judgment that proved better than that competence.

## Deterministic facts — run first

- the **genre → unit-of-work** mapping (`write-<genre>`) and the resulting composition, read from the
  engine, not decided here — the same "which composition" fact `frame` computes for any unit of work.
- the **style contract** from the brief, relayed verbatim into the draft's constraints so revision can
  later check the draft against it.

(No deterministic *craft* — there is nothing about the prose itself that is a fact rather than
judgment. If a genuinely mechanical writing aid appears — a structure template a genre always takes —
it is a candidate praxis script, left unbuilt until it is concrete, not guessed at.)

## The work

- Draft the piece to the genre's shape and hold the stated style throughout. One draft, whole enough
  for revision to work on — not polished, not self-edited to death (that is `writing-revision`'s job,
  and over-converging here defeats the divergent stance).
- Keep the draft's own scaffolding (an outline, alternative openings, notes-to-self) if it helps the
  reviser see the choices; strip it only at revision.

**Artifact:** the first draft, plus the style contract it was written against, handed forward to
`writing-revision`.

**Surfaced/lacking:** this is the judgment-capture seam. When the draft required a real writing
decision whose reasoning would guide the *next* piece better than baseline would — a genre convention
that actually changed the choice, a style tradeoff that recurred — surface it as a `proposals:`
candidate for the ratify gate. That is how a genre/style domain is *born*: from a decision worth
feeding forward, not from an org chart written up front. A genre the piece needed but no unit-of-work
names is a finding, not a silent stretch of an ill-fitting one.
