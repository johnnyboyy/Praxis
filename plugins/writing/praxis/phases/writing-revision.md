# Phase: writing-revision

Revise a prose-product draft toward its final form — against the genre's demands, the reader it is
for, and the style contract it was drafted under. The **convergent** half of the writing process,
following `writing-draft`. Distinct from corpora's own `prose-revision` (which tightens the artifacts
corpora *itself* produces — handoffs, specs, principles); this revises the prose *product* being
authored.

Like its sibling, this phase is **process, not craft**. It structures *how a draft converges* — read
against genre, reader, and style; cut and sharpen; decide done — without carrying pre-authored rules
about what good revision decides. Revision craft that beats baseline accretes at the gate, per genre.

**Entry condition:** a draft handed forward from `writing-draft` (or an existing prose product
submitted for revision, `revise-writing [target]`), carrying its genre and style contract.

**Stance:** convergent — it narrows an existing draft toward one finished piece; it does not open new
directions (a draft that turns out to need a different genre or a fresh direction goes back to
`writing-draft`, not forward here).

**Invocations:** the judgment engine composed for the draft's genre unit-of-work — the same
composition `writing-draft` used, now applied to sharpen rather than generate. Thin early; the reviser
works on its own competence and the gate keeps only what proved better.

## Deterministic facts — run first

- the **style contract** carried from the draft — revision checks the piece against it (did the voice,
  formality, person, tense hold), which is a concrete comparison, not a judgment about whether the
  style was the right one.
- the **genre unit-of-work** and its composition, read from the engine.

## The pass

- Read the whole draft once for **genre fit** (does it do what this kind of writing is for) and once
  for **reader fit** (does its intended reader get what they came for), then revise: cut what does not
  serve the piece, sharpen what does, and hold the style contract throughout.
- Converge to **done** — a finished piece, its draft scaffolding (outlines, alternatives, notes)
  removed. "Done" is a judgment the reviser commits to, not an infinite polish.

**Artifact:** the finished prose product.

**Surfaced/lacking:** the same judgment-capture seam as `writing-draft`. A revision decision whose
reasoning would sharpen the next piece — a recurring cut a genre always needs, a reader-fit test that
caught something baseline missed — is a `proposals:` candidate for the gate, and the seed of a genre
or style domain. A gap that is a real hole in the piece (missing content, not verbosity) is a
**finding** for the unit's `Surfaced` section, not something to invent here.
